# 05. The community pool and Polar's Acceptable Use Policy

Stage: decision support for D1 (`02-stripe-to-polar-mapping.md`). Nothing here changes code or copy; every change below is a proposal.

**Status (2026-10-05, later): D1 resolved — revenue-funded pool, no customer pool purchases.** The owner chose a variant of option D (§4): Tangent sells only the yearly membership ($10/year, with its included credit) and personal credit (provider cost + 10% markup), and publicly commits a share of its own revenue to the pool, which is an operator expense like a free tier. Every Polar sale is the buyer's own membership or prepaid usage, so the AUP question in this document no longer arises, and §6 need not be sent. What shipped:

- **Removed:** `target: 'pool'` checkouts (a 400 now), `POOL_PURCHASES_ENABLED`, `POOL_MIN_PURCHASE_CENTS`, the fund section and `#fund-pool`, `fundingOpen` / `markupBps` / `minPurchaseCents` on `GET /api/pool/status`, simulated pool purchases, "Funding opens soon" / "Buying credit for the pool isn't available yet". A Polar order whose metadata still says `target: 'pool'` maps to `unknown` and is never credited. Legacy pool purchase rows keep working for refunds and disputes (clamped).
- **The pool pays actual cost:** `POOL_MARKUP_BPS` is removed and pool replies carry no markup.
- **The commitment, `POOL_REVENUE_SHARE_BPS` (default 20%), quoted from config on every public surface:** "The community pool is free credit Tangent provides. Tangent puts 20% of what it earns into it: 20% of each membership payment after payment fees, and 20% of the markup on credit as it's used." Membership: 20% × (pre-tax amount − Polar's fee) per payment, reversed in proportion by refunds. Credit: 20% of the **markup** part of settled personal charges, once per UTC day by the cron — not of the purchase, since a $10 top-up earns Tangent only ≈ $0.84 as it is spent. See docs/DECISIONS.md, "Revenue-funded community pool".
- **Copy:** landing, `/pool` (with what the share added this week and month, and the operator's contact for questions or arrangements), the billing page's pool section, Learn's home card and payment dialog, the empty-pool notice, Terms §7 (operator-provided at our discretion, funded from revenue, may change or end; credit non-transferable, no cash value, refundable only where the law requires or under Polar's buyer terms), docs/LEGAL.md, README and SPEC.md.
- **Still open, cosmetic only:** the AUP-motivated renames in §3.2 ("community" → "shared", "supporters" → "credit buyers", "funded") were not made ("learners helped" became "learners on the pool" with the landing page rework); with no customer pool purchases they are no longer needed for the AUP.

The rest of this document is the analysis as written before that decision.

**Earlier status (2026-10-05):** the accuracy fixes are applied. Pool copy that said people fund the pool is now conditional on `fundingOpen`: while pool purchases are closed it says Tangent adds the pool's credit, offers nothing to buy and says "Buying credit for the pool isn't available yet" instead of "Funding opens soon" (landing, `/pool`, billing section, empty-pool notice, Learn home card and payment dialog, `poolEmptyText`). `/pool` no longer says a pool purchase is "from {operator}", the markup is described as Tangent's margin, the supporter paragraph states the real rule (any credit purchase) instead of "a thank-you for funding Tangent", Terms §7 describes pool purchases as "when pool credit purchases are offered", SPEC.md:19 and D15 are revised. The AUP-motivated renames ("community pool" → "shared pool", "supporters" → "credit buyers", dropping "fund"/"helped", the `#fund-pool` anchor and JSON fields) are deliberately deferred: the owner is researching a second payment provider that permits crowdfunding to process pool purchases, so the buyer-funded framing stays for the open state.

## 0. Summary

- **What Polar says** (fetched 2026-10-05):
  - Prohibited: "Donations, crowdfunding, community access, advertising, and sponsorship". Also prohibited: "services facilitating transactions, investments, or balances for customers" (financial services).
  - Restricted, with closer review: "AI Content Generation tools (text, image, video, voice)" and "Audio / music / chatbot generators".
  - Allowed: "Software & SaaS" and "Premium content & access".
  - Polar "reserves the right to refuse Services to sell any Product in its sole discretion". Where a use case is unclear, it says to contact support@polar.sh.
  - Account reviews look at the live website, the product descriptions and the checkout. A product that breaks the AUP gets the merchant offboarded with payouts held ([account reviews](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/account-reviews)).
- **What the pool is.** A buyer pays the merchant of record. The money becomes prepaid AI-usage credit that the buyer does not control, and that other people consume at no charge to them. The only thing the buyer gets in return is the "supporter" tier (higher daily pool limits), and **any** purchase gives that tier, including a $5 top-up for the buyer's own account. Rewording does not change this. A reasonable reviewer can read a pool purchase as a donation or crowdfunding for a free service, even though no money goes to a charity or a third party, and the operator earns on it like on any sale (a 5 % markup per reply).
- **What the copy gets wrong.** Many strings use words a reviewer pattern-matches to the prohibited list: "fund" and "funded by people", "learners helped", "a thank-you for funding Tangent", "Supporters", "community", "Funding opens soon". On top of that, Polar's checkout and receipt show the same generic credits product for a pool purchase, so the order never says what was bought. The design spec also says the purchase framing was chosen partly to _avoid_ the processor's donation and crowdfunding review (`docs/pool/SPEC.md:19`). That sentence must not stand next to a request for Polar's approval, because the repository is public.
- **Recommendation.**
  1. Launch the pool **operator-funded** (option D, which needs nothing new from Polar), with copy that says so.
  2. Make the accuracy fixes in §3 that apply under any option.
  3. Send Polar the frank message in §6. It describes the current design and asks about the share-from-own-credit variant (option B).
  4. Turn on buyer-funded pool credit only with Polar's written OK, and then sell it as a **separate, clearly named Polar product**.
  5. If Polar says no, treat that answer as covering B as well, unless Polar explicitly says otherwise. Don't build B as a workaround.

How the prohibited terms apply:

- "Community access" sits next to an _allowed_ "Discord server access", so it most likely means selling membership of a community. The pool is not that: buying pool credit gives no access to anything a non-buyer lacks, except the supporter limits. The word "community" in the product name still invites the match.
- "Balances for customers": the pool holds a shared balance, but it is non-cash and non-redeemable, and only the operator's own service can spend it. That is low risk, but worth stating plainly to Polar.

## 3. Assessment

### 3.1 Substance: what rewording can't change

1. **The buyer pays and others consume.** A pool purchase gives the buyer nothing to consume. The credit "belongs to the pool, not to you" (Terms §7). In ordinary language that is paying for other people's use of a service, which is the core of "donation" and "crowdfunding". What separates it from both:
   - The money buys the seller's own service, at the seller's normal price plus markup. No cause, no third party and no campaign target is involved, and nothing is passed through.
   - The operator profits per reply.
   - The credit has no cash value and can be spent only on Tangent.

   It is closest to **buying prepaid service for unnamed others** (a "suspended coffee" or a gift of service time). Whether Polar treats that as allowed SaaS or as prohibited donations is their call. Copy can't settle it.

2. **The supporter tier is not a pool perk.** `pool/supporter.ts` counts **any** purchase, personal or pool, and admin credit never counts. So it is a paying-customer tier, not a sponsorship reward. This is a real strength, but the label "supporter" and the line "It's a thank-you for funding Tangent" (`pool-page.ts:188`) turn it back into sponsorship language. A pool buyer gets exactly what a $5 personal top-up gets. Don't present the tier as what the pool buyer is paying for: that would be inaccurate.
3. **Free consumption for non-buyers.** For the learner it is a free tier, and a free tier is unremarkable SaaS. The only unusual part is _who_ pays for it.
4. **The order is undifferentiated (#66).** Pool and personal purchases use one Polar product, so Polar's checkout, receipts and order list don't show which orders were pool purchases. Fix this _if_ pool purchases are approved. It serves Polar, the buyer (a receipt that says what they bought) and refunds.

### 3.2 Wording: what to fix (accurate, not concealing)

Principles:

- Say what happens: "buy credit for the shared pool", "the pool paid for N replies".
- Drop charity, sponsorship and crowdfunding vocabulary: "helped", "funded by people", "thank-you for funding", "supporters", "contribute", "refills as people fund it".
- Keep every disclosure: who can spend it, that the buyer can't take it back, the markup, the fee.
- Don't hide the pool from Polar or from buyers.

### 3.3 Polar product and checkout (if pool purchases are approved)

- A **second one-time Polar product**, e.g. "Tangent Learn – shared pool credit", with description:
  > "Prepaid AI-tutoring usage credit added to Tangent Learn's shared pool, which any signed-in Tangent Learn user can use within daily limits. You can't spend it on your own account or get it back as cash. Not a donation; not tax-deductible."
- With that product, the checkout page, receipt, invoice and Polar's order list all say what was bought.
- Code cost is about half a day:
  - a `POLAR_POOL_CREDITS_PRODUCT_ID` var (config, env and wrangler);
  - `createTopUpCheckout` picks the product by `input.target`;
  - README.
- `map.ts` needs no change, because credits orders are recognised by `metadata.kind`, not by product id (`map.ts:88`).
- Keep the existing metadata.

### 3.4 Copy guard

`FORBIDDEN_POOL_COPY` (`/donat|donor|tax[- ]?deductible|charit/i`) currently guards against _calling_ the purchase a donation. Its purpose should be accuracy, not avoiding trigger words. Extend it to `/donat|donor|tax[- ]?deductible|charit|sponsor|crowdfund|patron|give back|helped/i`. Don't remove words like "pool" or the honest disclosures to satisfy it.

## 4. Structural options

### A. Status quo: buyer chooses the destination at checkout (reworded, separate product)

- **What it is:** today's design, with §3.2 and §3.3 applied.
- **AUP fit:** depends on Polar. It is the most "pay for others" of the options, so it is the most likely to be read as donations or crowdfunding.
- **Buyer value:** none they consume. They get the paid-account tier, which a personal purchase also gives.
- **Cost:** about 1.5 days (copy plus product).
- **Risks:**
  - A wrong guess means offboarding with payouts held, which also takes down personal top-ups and the membership.
  - Refunds and disputes of pool purchases need clawbacks from a pool that may already have spent the credit (`pool_debit_shortfall`, absorbed by the operator).

### B. Buy for yourself, then share credit to the pool

- **What it is:**
  - Every Polar purchase is personal prepaid credit, the clear-cut Polar "Credits" pattern.
  - In the app, a user can move part of their **own unspent balance** to the shared pool: "Share $5 of my credit with the pool". The move is irrevocable.
  - `target: 'pool'` checkouts are removed.
- **AUP fit:** better on paper. Every Polar transaction is a purchase for the buyer's own account, and sharing happens later, inside the product.
  - Honest caveat: the money still ends up paying for other people's usage. It just takes one more step. If Polar's objection is to the substance (the buyer pays, others consume), B doesn't cure it.
  - So B must be disclosed and asked about (§6). It must not be adopted as a way around a "no".
- **Buyer value:**
  - The buyer has a real choice after buying, and can keep the credit.
  - It works better for a buyer who wants both.
  - It cleanly separates "buy" (commerce) from "share" (use of one's own goods).
- **Cost:** about 3-5 days in this codebase.
  - A `POST /api/pool/share` route and schema in `@tangent/shared`.
  - A transfer:
    - a debit grant on `u_<userId>` that must respect pending personal holds (the meter's balance check, in one D1 batch with a conditional insert);
    - a positive grant on the pool account (positive pool grants need no PoolBank lock);
    - one idempotency key for both rows;
    - a new grant kind, e.g. `share` (the `credit_grants.kind` enum in `db/schema.ts:351`), plus a Drizzle snapshot.
  - Refund policy:
    - a refunded purchase whose credit was shared debits the buyer's personal balance, which may go negative;
    - the pool is not clawed back. That is simpler than today and moves the shortfall from the operator to the buyer's balance.
  - Terms §7: "can't be transferred" gets an exception for sharing to the pool.
  - UI: a share control on the billing page, replacing the fund section.
  - Keep the supporter rule (purchases only; shares don't count separately).
  - Tests: ledger, meter race, refund after share, idempotency.
- **Risks:**
  - It is more code.
  - It could look like structuring around the policy if not disclosed.

### C. Gift credit to a named person

- **What it is:** the buyer purchases credit for a specific recipient (gift code or email).
- **AUP fit:** gift purchases are ordinary commerce, but they reintroduce stored-value and gift-card concerns: state gift-card and escheat rules, and the "balances for customers" line.
- **Buyer value:** clear, and the gift is personal.
- **Cost:** high, 1-2 weeks plus legal: codes, redemption, expiry rules, fraud (stolen cards buying codes), and emails.
- **Fit with the goal:** poor. It does not give an open pool for strangers.
- **Verdict:** not recommended.

### D. Operator-funded pool, with purchases only for the buyer's own account

- **What it is:**
  - The pool is Tangent's free tier. Only the operator adds credit to it: admin adjustments today, optionally an automatic monthly allocation, e.g. a share of the markup earned on personal credit.
  - Users buy credit only for themselves. Any purchase still raises their pool limits (the existing paid-account tier).
  - The meter, impact feed, caps, Turnstile and consent all stay.
- **AUP fit:** clean. A free tier funded by the vendor is ordinary SaaS, and every sale is the buyer's own prepaid usage.
- **Buyer value:** their own credit, plus higher free-tier limits.
- **Cost:**
  - Code: none. This is today's behaviour with `POOL_PURCHASES_ENABLED="false"`.
  - Copy: about 1 day (the D column in §3.5; hide "Funding opens soon" and the fund section while purchases are off).
  - Optional cron allocation: about 1 day (a monthly admin-style grant keyed `alloc:<YYYY-MM>`).
- **Trade-offs:**
  - It gives up the spec's "community-funded" goal and the "visibility drives funding" loop.
  - The operator carries the cost, bounded by what they choose to add. The pool can never go negative (SPEC reasoning 1), so the exposure is exactly the allocation.

### E. A separate processor for pool purchases

- **What it is:** D1's fallback.
- **AUP fit:** the same question comes up with any processor; Stripe also restricts crowdfunding and donations for non-charities.
- **Cost:** reintroduces a second payment provider behind the `PaymentProvider` port. That is days of work plus compliance, and a second MoR or tax setup.
- **Verdict:** not recommended unless pool funding becomes central to the business.

### 4.1 Recommendation

1. **Now, under any option:**
   - Fix the inaccurate or charity-flavoured wording: "helped", "thank-you for funding Tangent", "supporters", "funded by people", "from {operator}" (it is Polar under MoR), "Funding opens soon".
   - Replace the last sentence of SPEC.md:19.
   - Revise D15 so the account review mentions the pool.
2. **Launch the pool as option D.** Keep `POOL_PURCHASES_ENABLED="false"`, use the D copy and fund the pool by admin adjustment. It is accurate today, because nobody can buy pool credit.
3. **Send §6 to Polar.** It describes A as built, offers B as an alternative and asks which, if either, is acceptable, in writing.
4. **On the answer:**
   - **A approved:** create the separate pool product (§3.3), switch to the A copy and enable the flag.
   - **Only B approved:** build B, then enable sharing.
   - **No:** stay on D permanently. Don't build B unless Polar's answer explicitly allows it.
